Funkle privacy policy

Effective 23 October 2026 · applies to the Funkle Android app and web page (the app was called TAV until October 2026; the package id io.github.christianernst.tav is unchanged)

Funkle turns the music playing on your device into light. It is built so that the music itself never leaves your device, and so that the app works fully without an account. This page says what the app does see, what it stores, and how you get rid of it.

The short version

  • Audio is analysed on your device, in memory, and never recorded, stored or sent anywhere.
  • Song titles, artists and album art come from the player on your device and stay there.
  • The app starts as an anonymous account so that your settings and saved looks can follow you. You can add an email address or a Google account; you never have to.
  • Anonymous usage events (which scene, which setting, how long) help improve the app. One switch turns them off, one link deletes them.
  • Everything the cloud holds lives on Supabase servers in the European Union, under your own account id. No advertising, no tracking across apps or sites, no selling of data.

Audio

On Android, Funkle listens to the device's audio output through Android's Visualizer interface, or to the microphone if you choose that, which is why it asks for the record audio permission. The samples are turned into levels, bands and beats on the device and are discarded frame by frame. Nothing is recorded, nothing is sent. In the browser, Funkle uses the Web Audio API on a file or stream you pick, again entirely on your device.

Now playing

With your permission (Android's notification access), Funkle reads the media session of your music player, such as Spotify, to show the song title and progress, to colour the visuals from the album art, and, if you turn that on, to offer a silent notification when music starts. That data is used on the device and is not sent to our servers or anyone else. Funkle does not use the Spotify API and has no access to your Spotify account.

Accounts

When the cloud is enabled, the app signs in anonymously on first start and is given a random account id. If you add an email address (magic link) or sign in with Google, that same account becomes yours. From Google we receive only your email address and basic profile (name, picture), which we use to identify your account. Supabase, our authentication and database provider, processes this on our behalf. We do not read your contacts, calendar, Drive or anything else.

Usage events never contain audio, song titles, artists, your location or the contents of any other app. Collection can be turned off at any time with the Help improve Funkle with anonymous usage stats switch in the account card, and Delete my usage history removes what was collected so far.

Where it is stored

All cloud data is stored by Supabase in the eu-west-1 region (Ireland, European Union). Transfers between the app and the servers are encrypted (HTTPS). Row-level security on the database means each account can only read and write its own rows.

Sharing a look

Sharing produces a link or a six-letter code that contains the look's name, colours and settings, and nothing about you. Anyone with the link can open it. A look you make public can be found by other users; you can make it private or delete it again from the Looks card.

Deleting your data

  • Usage events: account card → Delete my usage history. Immediate.
  • Saved looks: delete them from the Looks card.
  • Your account and everything in it: send a request from the email address on the account to the support address shown on the Google sign-in screen and on the app's store listing, or open an issue at github.com/Christian-Ernst/TAV/issues. The account, its settings, looks and events are removed within 30 days.
  • Uninstalling the app removes everything stored on the device.

Children

Funkle is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.
